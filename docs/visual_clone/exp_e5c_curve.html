e5c_curve — with how few tasks does L3 still work?

This experiment trains L3 on a random task fraction F of the 136 tasks and scores the held-out complement, for F in 0.10 to 0.90 and 40 seeds per fraction. It answers the data-efficiency question E5 left open: how small can the training task set be? It reuses the split, fit and statistics machinery of exp_e5b_splits.html verbatim.

Design — hypothesis, inputs, stages, artifacts

Diagram 1 — 12 boxes. Mint pills = functions in e5c_curve.py (plus the reused e5b_splits.load_all), dashed grey = frozen inputs, cyan = written artifacts. run_fraction (L53) loops the 40 seeds of SEEDS (L42) for one F of FRACTIONS (L41), calls e5b_splits.group_split with hold-fraction 1-F, then select_C_inner and fit_eval. fraction_block (L84) summarises 40 seeds into one row. Four consumers take that row list: min_viable_f (L113), saturation_f (L128), log2_reconciliation (L137) and make_plot (L163). write_md (L343) renders the Markdown digest; every scorer and C-grid stage is inherited from e5b_splits.py.
